The first fish is never the one you expect. It's small, it's surprised, and it comes up on the line to a shriek from whoever is holding the rod. Then there's a long, serious silence while everyone leans over a bucket of creek water to look at it.

That's the whole appeal of fishing from our dock. You don't need a boat, a guide or a plan. You need a few steps down from the porch, a bucket, and a child who has never felt a tug on a line.

## A bucket, a worm, a bottom rig

The setup is the simplest one there is. A bottom rig, a couple of pieces of worm, a line dropped off the end of the dock. Along the creeks and small rivers of the Northern Neck, that's how people have introduced kids to fishing for a long time, and the fish it tends to bring up are the small, scrappy ones: spot, perch, croaker. Nobody on the dock is chasing a trophy. They're chasing the next tug.

Bring a rod, bring a bit of patience, and let the youngest hold it first. Crabbing is the other thing the dock is good for, and if the kids get hooked on one they'll want the other. (<a href={withBase("/journal/crabbers-morning-on-hull-creek")}>Crabbing at first light</a> is a good morning of its own.) If you're packing for a spring or summer weekend, the <a href={withBase("/what-to-bring")}>what-to-bring list</a> has room for a rod and a small tackle box.

## Where the time goes

A fishing session with kids is rarely about the fish. It's the bucket they refuse to empty, the worm that gets named, the heron that stands very still on the far bank while everyone pretends not to watch it. Somebody wades in at the shore. Somebody falls asleep in a chair. The tide slides out and the creek shows its sandy edge, and the afternoon slips past without anyone checking the time.

By evening the bucket is empty and the fish are back in the creek, and there's a hot tub on the patio beside the porch for the grown-ups and a fire pit for everyone.

## One paragraph on the rules

Virginia asks anglers 16 and older who don't hold a saltwater license to register, free of charge, with the Fisherman Identification Program run by the Virginia Marine Resources Commission, and children under 16 don't need to. The state exempts people fishing from private property they own or rent from buying a license, though whether that covers a rental stay is a quick question for VMRC, and we're happy to point you to it.
