## Property taxes: lower than you think, but the reassessment changed things

Northumberland County's FY2025-2026 property tax rate is **$0.74 per $100 of
assessed value**, up $0.08 from the prior year after a county-wide
reassessment effective January 1, 2026. The rate itself is modest: the county's
effective rate of 0.61% of market value sits well below the national median of
1.02%.

The thing to understand about the 2025 reassessment: Northumberland brought
assessed values to 100% of market value for the first time in years. For a lot
of waterfront owners, the assessed value jumped even as the nominal rate held
roughly flat. The math is simple: assessed value ÷ 100, then × $0.74. On a
property assessed at $400,000 that's $2,960 a year. On $450,000, it's $3,330.

## Flood insurance: the number most buyers underestimate

Hull Creek is a tidal tributary of the Potomac, which means waterfront
properties here sit in FEMA Special Flood Hazard territory. NFIP premiums in
Virginia under Risk Rating 2.0 run $250 to $1,973 a year for Zone AE, with the
state median around $664. Properties at or near base flood elevation (which
describes most waterfront lots with dock access) tend toward the upper half of
that range. Figure $900 to $1,600 as a realistic planning number for a property
like this one if you don't yet have your own Elevation Certificate.

Two things buyers miss: first, flood coverage is not in your standard homeowners
policy. It's a completely separate policy. You will write two checks. Second,
new NFIP policies have a 30-day waiting period before they take effect, so you
can't buy coverage the week before a named storm and expect it to stick.

## Homeowners insurance: the wind deductible deserves its own line

Virginia is one of 19 states where hurricane and named-storm deductibles
work differently from the base policy deductible. The typical range is 1% to 5%
of dwelling coverage, applied separately, before the main policy kicks in. On
a dwelling insured for $300,000, a 5% hurricane deductible means $15,000 out
of pocket before a single cent of coverage flows. That number should be in your
budget, not a surprise.

Standard homeowners premiums for coastal Virginia properties run meaningfully
higher than inland: regional data shows coastal VA can run $1,900 to $2,400 a
year depending on exposure. The Northern Neck sits inland enough from the ocean
that full coastal exposure isn't the whole story, but it's still waterfront, and
insurers price it that way.

## The dock: the line item that catches people off guard

A dock is the reason you're buying waterfront, and it's also the thing that
will spend money regularly and ask for more every few years. Routine annual
maintenance: cleaning, board inspection, hardware, runs $200 to $1,200
depending on what you find. A moderate repair incident (replacing boards, fixing
a cleat, repairing a section of decking) averages around $3,376 nationally.
Major structural work, including piling replacement, can run $10,000 to $15,000
or beyond.

Hull Creek is brackish, which is gentler than full saltwater but still harder
on wood and hardware than a freshwater lake. Treated wood pilings that once lasted 30-plus years may run only 10 to 15 years in saltwater environments; brackish water like Hull Creek is gentler, but it still accelerates wear compared to fresh water, and Will's direct experience with these pilings is the more useful data point than any national average. We have the crab pot, the kayaks,
guests in the water off the dock end. It earns its keep, and it costs accordingly.

## HVAC, septic, and well: the rural waterfront operating triad

These three tend to get lumped together in "other" on a pro forma and then
surprise you individually.

**HVAC:** A full system replacement in Virginia runs $7,500 on average, with
heat pump systems ranging from $6,800 to $21,000 for the full install. Coastal
and brackish environments are harder on condenser coils and outdoor units than
inland air, worth factoring into your maintenance schedule and expected system
lifespan.

**Septic:** Northumberland County sits in the Chesapeake Bay Preservation Area,
which means the Virginia Department of Health requires pump-outs at least every
five years. A standard pump runs $350 to $650 in Virginia. A full system
replacement, the scenario you plan against and hope never arrives, runs
$10,000 to $30,000 or more depending on soil and system type. A Chesapeake Bay
location may require an enhanced alternative system if the original fails, which
pushes that higher end further up.

## The HOA question (and why the Northern Neck answer is good)

Most of the rural Northern Neck waterfront market has no HOA. That's a genuine
financial and use-freedom advantage over resort beach communities where an HOA
can run $500 to $2,000 a year and comes with a list of things you can't do
with your own dock. The absence of an HOA is not a small thing.

## The honest answer to "is it worth it?"

The real cost of owning a waterfront cottage on the Northern Neck is the sum of
a property tax bill that's lower than most of the country, an insurance stack
(homeowners plus flood, separate policies, one with a significant wind
deductible), and a maintenance rhythm dominated by the dock, the systems, and
the things that brackish air does to metal and wood over time. None of it is
cheap. None of it is surprising if you know it's coming.

What I'd tell a friend running the numbers: the list price is the honest start,
not the end. Get quotes on flood insurance before you make an offer, not after.
Ask to see the last septic pump-out record. Walk the dock in person and look at
the pilings. Then decide, with clear eyes, whether you want this life.
