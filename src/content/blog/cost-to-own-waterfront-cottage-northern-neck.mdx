## What I'd tell a friend running the numbers

The carrying costs break into four real buckets: taxes and insurance (together,
the fixed annual stack), the dock, the systems (HVAC, septic, well), and the
pleasant surprise that there's no HOA. None of it is cheap. None of it is
surprising if you know it's coming — and that's the whole point of this post.

**Taxes** in Northumberland County are lower than most of the country. The
FY2025-2026 rate is $0.74 per $100 of assessed value, which puts the effective
rate well below the national median of 1.02%. The thing to understand after the
2025 reassessment: the county brought assessed values to 100% of market value
for the first time in years, so the math changed even where the rate held
roughly flat. On a property assessed at $400,000, you're looking at $2,960 a
year; at $450,000, it's $3,330.

**Flood insurance** is the one that catches people. Hull Creek is a tidal
tributary of the Potomac, and waterfront properties here typically sit in FEMA
Special Flood Hazard territory. The key fact most buyers miss: flood coverage
isn't in your standard homeowners policy. It's a separate policy, a separate
check. Under FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0, individual premiums are driven by
elevation, foundation type, and distance to water — the flood zone tells you
roughly where you sit but doesn't tell you what you'll pay. Get a quote before
you make an offer, not after.

**Homeowners insurance** adds a wrinkle that doesn't show up in most
checklists: the hurricane deductible. Virginia is one of 19 states where
named-storm deductibles work separately from the base policy. Typical range is
1%–5% of dwelling coverage, applied first, before standard coverage begins. On
a $300,000-insured dwelling at 5%, that's $15,000 out of pocket. It should be
in your budget, not a surprise on a claim form.

**The dock** is the reason you're buying waterfront, and it'll spend money
regularly and ask for more every few years. Hull Creek is brackish —
gentler than full saltwater but harder on wood and hardware than a fresh lake.
We've got the crab pot, the kayaks, guests in the water off the dock end
through the summer. It earns its keep. Budget routine annual maintenance plus a
reserve for the day a piling needs work; that day comes for every dock on this
creek.

**HVAC, septic, and well** are the rural waterfront operating triad. They get
lumped into "other" on a pro forma and then surprise you individually.

HVAC: coastal air — even brackish — is harder on outdoor condenser coils than
inland air, worth factoring into your maintenance schedule and expected system
lifespan.

Septic: Northumberland County sits in the Chesapeake Bay Preservation Area,
which means Virginia requires pump-outs at least every five years. A standard
pump runs $350–$650 in Virginia; full system replacement — the scenario you
plan against and hope never arrives — can run $10,000 to $30,000 or more,
higher if the Bay Preservation rules require an enhanced alternative system.

which is a genuine financial and use-freedom advantage over resort beach
