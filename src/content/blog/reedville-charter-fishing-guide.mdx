The best fishing day of a stay here starts at a marina fifteen minutes from the front door. You pour coffee at the cottage while it's still dim, drive to Reedville, and by the time the sun is up you're out on the Chesapeake Bay with a captain who knows where the fish were yesterday.

We don't run the boats. We know a few of the local captains who do, and that's the best way to book. Tell us before your stay and we'll connect you.

## How a Reedville charter day goes

Reedville is a working fishing town at the mouth of the Bay, and the charter boats tie up at Buzzards Point Marina on the edge of it. You show up, you meet the captain, you climb aboard. The boat, the tackle and the know-how are the captain's. Yours is the cooler, a hat, and a willingness to let a stranger tell you where to stand. (If you'd like a look at the town before or after, our <a href={withBase("/journal/complete-guide-to-reedville-virginia")}>guide to Reedville</a> covers the Fishermen's Museum, Main Street and the ice cream.)

Trips come in a few lengths. A short sunset cruise, a half day, a full day on the water. One captain we looked at lists four-hour trips starting at $450 and eight-hour days starting at $850, though prices change, so ask when you book. Charters launch from that marina through much of the year, with striped bass, bluefish and cobia among the fish the Bay is known for, and the captain will tell you what's running the week you come.

## Booking it

Book early for the weekends you care about. Spring and summer weekends fill, and a captain with an open boat is a captain you want to have already spoken to. Send us a note once your dates are set and we'll point you to a few names.

The practical side is short. Paying passengers on a licensed charter don't need their own fishing license, so confirm the boat is licensed when you book. The captain also carries the rules on sizes and limits, which is one less thing to read on the drive.

## Bring the day home

This is the part we like best. You come back up the road with a cooler, and the kitchen is waiting: modest, with everything you need and nothing you don't, good counter space, and a window over the water. Our <a href={withBase("/journal/waterfront-kitchen-family-recipes")}>kitchen notes</a> have a few easy dinners for the evening. Afterward there's the hot tub on the patio beside the porch, and a cedar sauna if anyone's sore from a day of reeling.
