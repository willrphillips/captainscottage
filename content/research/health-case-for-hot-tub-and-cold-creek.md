### From external research (health literature — hedge all claims)
- Contrast hydrotherapy (alternating hot/cold water exposure): widely practiced in Finnish and Nordic sauna tradition; used in sports medicine for post-exercise recovery. Not a new concept; well-documented as a wellness practice, though rigorous RCT evidence varies by outcome.
- Hot water immersion effects: promotes vasodilation (blood vessels widen), muscle relaxation, parasympathetic nervous system activation (rest-and-digest). These are broadly accepted physiological descriptions, not contested. Still: hedge with "may," "can support," etc. Do not make medical claims.
- Cold water immersion effects: promotes vasoconstriction, triggers norepinephrine and endorphin release, associated with mood lift and alertness. Study quality varies (n=33 studies and observational cohorts exist). Hedge: "some people report," "associated with," not "causes."
- The combination (contrast therapy): the alternating hot/cold cycle — vasodilation then vasoconstriction — is the mechanism cited in sports recovery literature for improved circulation and perceived fatigue reduction. UNVERIFIED whether effects are meaningfully different for non-athletes in wellness settings vs. clinical populations. Do not overstate.
