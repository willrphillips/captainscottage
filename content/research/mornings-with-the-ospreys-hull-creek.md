### Osprey seasonality — external, verified

- Ospreys arrive at Chesapeake Bay nest sites in **early March** (experienced breeders sometimes late February); breeding season runs **March through August**; they begin migrating south in **mid-August**.  
  Source: Chesapeake Bay Program field guide — [https://www.chesapeakebay.net/discover/field-guide/entry/osprey](https://www.chesapeakebay.net/discover/field-guide/entry/osprey)

- Approximately one-quarter of all osprey nests in the contiguous United States are in the Chesapeake Bay watershed — making this one of the densest osprey populations in North America.  
  Source: Chesapeake Bay Program field guide — [https://www.chesapeakebay.net/discover/field-guide/entry/osprey](https://www.chesapeakebay.net/discover/field-guide/entry/osprey)

- In lower-salinity areas of the Bay system — including the upper Potomac and Rappahannock Rivers — osprey breeding success is measurably stronger than in higher-salinity zones. Hull Creek, as a mostly-fresh brackish tributary of the Potomac, sits in this better-performing zone.  
  Source: WHRO / Center for Conservation Biology, 2025 study summary — [https://www.whro.org/environment/2025-10-02/massive-new-study-says-ospreys-are-thriving-in-some-parts-of-the-chesapeake-bay-but-not-enough](https://www.whro.org/environment/2025-10-02/massive-new-study-says-ospreys-are-thriving-in-some-parts-of-the-chesapeake-bay-but-not-enough)

- Ospreys have roughly **70% fishing accuracy** ("hover, fold wings, and dive").  
  Source: Virginia DCR State Parks blog — [https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/state-parks/blog/osprey-prey-season](https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/state-parks/blog/osprey-prey-season)

- Virginia DCR notes ospreys typically arrive in the first two weeks of March at Tidewater sites (York River State Park cited as reference).  
  Source: Virginia DCR State Parks blog — [https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/state-parks/blog/osprey-prey-season](https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/state-parks/blog/osprey-prey-season)

- **Do not overstate the chick-starvation research** (2025 studies found some osprey chicks are starving in high-salinity Bay zones). That is a conservation note, not a journal post beat. Hull Creek's lower-salinity status actually makes it a relatively good news story. Leave the negative data out entirely.

### Bald eagle seasonality — external, verified

- Bald eagles in the Chesapeake Bay include a **year-round resident population** (locally raised birds typically stay if water access is available), a migratory summer population from the Southeast, and a migratory winter population from the Northeast.  
  Source: Chesapeake Bay Program field guide — [https://www.chesapeakebay.net/discover/field-guide/entry/bald-eagle](https://www.chesapeakebay.net/discover/field-guide/entry/bald-eagle)

- The **Potomac River** is specifically identified by the Chesapeake Bay Program as one of the major bald eagle breeding hotspot corridors ("forested shorelines" cited), alongside the James and Rappahannock. Large breeding populations are established along all three rivers.  
  Source: Chesapeake Bay Program field guide — [https://www.chesapeakebay.net/discover/field-guide/entry/bald-eagle](https://www.chesapeakebay.net/discover/field-guide/entry/bald-eagle)

- An estimated **3,000 breeding pairs of bald eagles** in the Chesapeake Bay region (2020 estimate). The Northern Neck, lying between the Potomac and Rappahannock, is described as hosting "some of the largest concentrations of bald eagles on the Eastern Seaboard."  
  Source: Virginia DWR / VBWT Northern Neck loop page — [https://dwr.virginia.gov/vbwt/coastal-trail/cnn/](https://dwr.virginia.gov/vbwt/coastal-trail/cnn/)

- For the post, "bald eagle" can be described simply as a year-round presence on this part of the Potomac. No need to qualify with seasonal caveats — they are genuinely there in all seasons.

### Atlantic Flyway / regional birding context — external, verified

- Virginia's **Virginia Bird & Wildlife Trail (VBWT)** has a dedicated **Northern Neck loop (CNN)** with 22 sites, several of them in Northumberland County (Dameron Marsh Natural Area Preserve, Hughlett Point Natural Area Preserve, and others). The loop covers the area between the Potomac and Rappahannock rivers where they meet the Bay.  
  Source: Virginia DWR VBWT Northern Neck loop — [https://dwr.virginia.gov/vbwt/coastal-trail/cnn/](https://dwr.virginia.gov/vbwt/coastal-trail/cnn/)

- The Northern Neck lies on the **Atlantic Flyway** migration corridor. Each spring and fall, raptors, waders, and neotropical songbirds pass through Virginia along this flyway.  
  Source: Virginia DWR — [https://dwr.virginia.gov/blog/spring-migration-has-begun-visit-the-virginia-birding-wildlife-trail-to-view-migratory-birds/](https://dwr.virginia.gov/blog/spring-migration-has-begun-visit-the-virginia-birding-wildlife-trail-to-view-migratory-birds/)

- **For the post:** these facts ground the piece without turning it into a guide. One way to use them: a single light mention that this stretch of water sits inside one of the most bird-dense corridors on the East Coast — that's the reason a morning here feels different from a morning on a suburban pond. Don't list the trail or name the flyway explicitly unless it flows naturally.

### Great blue heron & egret — behavior notes (no specific source required; these are well-established field observations)

- Great blue herons are solitary hunters; they stand motionless in shallows waiting for fish. Their silhouette at dawn — still, prehistoric-looking — is a strong visual detail.  
  UNVERIFIED as a site-specific claim — but general heron behavior is not in dispute. Use as a reliable sensory description, not a statistic.

- Belted Kingfisher — identified by its rattling call before it is seen; low, straight, fast flight along the water's edge; perches on dock pilings or overhanging branches before diving.  
  UNVERIFIED as a site-specific claim — but standard kingfisher behavior, widely documented. Safe to describe in scene-setting prose.
