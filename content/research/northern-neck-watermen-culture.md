### From external research
- Virginia's Chesapeake Bay watermen: one of the oldest continuous working maritime cultures in North America. Commercial fishing, crabbing, and oystering date to at least the 17th century on the Chesapeake. Reasonably verified as historical consensus.
- Watermen's tools and vessels: pound-net boats, crab scrape skiffs, buyboats (wide-bottomed vessels that collect catch from smaller boats and deliver to processors — a distinctive Chesapeake vessel type), patent-tong boats (oyster harvesting). Buyboats are particularly characteristic of the Northern Neck / middle Chesapeake. UNVERIFIED whether buyboats are still commercially active on Hull Creek specifically — describe as visible on the broader Potomac/Chesapeake waterways.
- Menhaden significance: Reedville's menhaden industry made it the wealthiest per capita town in the US in the late 1800s (commonly cited by the Reedville Fishermen's Museum). - Blue crab season: commercial and recreational crabbing on the Chesapeake, April through November; peak June–September. Watermen pull pots before dawn daily during the season. Reasonably verified.
