### Founding and history
- Reedville was founded in 1874 by Captain Elijah W. Reed (1827–1888), a sea captain from Brooklin, Maine. He purchased land on Cockrell Creek and opened the first menhaden processing plant in the Northern Neck. Source: [Wikipedia — Reedville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reedville,_Virginia); [Wikipedia — Elijah W. Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elijah_W._Reed).
- Reed's method extracted oil from menhaden by the millions; the oil was used as a lubricant and in lamp lighting (a whale-oil substitute), and the pressed carcasses became fertilizer. This was industrial, not artisanal — factories, not small boats. Source: Wikipedia (above); [Reedville Historical Marker — HMDB](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=22959).
- By the 1880s Reedville was the wealthiest town per capita in the United States, driven by being the nation's leading menhaden port. The town of approximately 500 people held that per-capita income distinction for years. Source: [Only in Your State — Reedville wealthiest town](https://www.onlyinyourstate.com/state-pride/virginia/reedville-wealthiest-town-us-va); [Wikipedia — Reedville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reedville,_Virginia).
- Reedville was the number-one U.S. port for commercial fishing tonnage for over 125 years. Source: [Rivah Visitor's Guide — Decades of Prosperity](https://www.rivahguide.com/decades-of-prosperity-the-history-of-reedville/).
- The town was officially named Reedville upon Captain Reed's death in 1888; his son George became the first postmaster. Source: Rivah Visitor's Guide (above).
- At peak, fifteen menhaden factories operated on Cockrell Creek simultaneously. Source: [Wikipedia — Reedville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reedville,_Virginia) (per the Grokipedia summary corroborating the 1885 figure).

### Millionaire's Row
- Boat captains and factory owners built Victorian-era mansions along Main Street with their menhaden fortunes. The street is known as "Millionaire's Row." Source: [Rivah Visitor's Guide — Decades of Prosperity](https://www.rivahguide.com/decades-of-prosperity-the-history-of-reedville/); [SAH Archipedia — Reedville](https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/VA-01-PE24).
- The Reedville Historic District and two of the Fishermen's Museum's vessels are listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Source: Wikipedia (above).
- The Morris House (circa 1895), a waterfront Victorian on Cockrell Creek, is frequently cited as a representative example of the era's architecture. Source: [The Old House Life — Morris House listing](https://theoldhouselife.com/2021/07/17/look-inside-the-waterfront-historic-morris-house-circa-1895-in-virginia-has-a-guest-house-1500000/); [Chesapeake Bay Magazine — Morris House](https://www.chesapeakebaymagazine.com/homes/liz-moore-morris-house/).

### Menhaden industry today (Omega Protein)
- Omega Protein Corporation operates the last remaining menhaden processing facility on the East Coast, on Cockrell Creek at Reedville. The facility has operated continuously since 1878. Source: [CLUI — Omega Protein Plant, Port of Reedville](https://clui.org/ludb/site/omega-protein-plant-port-reedville); [Wikipedia — Omega Protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega_Protein).
- As of January 2025 a federal court dismissed a challenge and Omega Protein's fishing division (Ocean Harvesters) was allowed to continue operating on the Chesapeake. Source: [SSentinel.com — Company plans to continue menhaden fishing](https://www.ssentinel.com/company-plans-to-continue-menhaden-fishing-in-chesapeake/).
- The industrial "Stack" (smokestack at the Omega Protein plant) is visible from Main Street and from the water — it is the single object that proves the industry never entirely left. Do not skip this detail; it is what separates Reedville from a generic preserved-Victorian town. Source: Rivah Visitor's Guide (above).

### Cockrell Creek and the waterfront walk
- Reedville sits at the terminus of U.S. Route 360 at the head of Cockrell Creek on the western shore of the Chesapeake Bay. Source: Wikipedia — Reedville, Virginia.
- Main Street runs the length of the slender peninsula between Cockrell Creek and Great Wicomico River, so almost every block has water on at least one side. The Victorian houses, the museum dock, Reedville Market's outdoor decks, and Chitterchats are all walkable from each other. Source: Rivah Visitor's Guide; SAH Archipedia; museum website.
- The Omega Protein plant is visible from the water end of Main Street — working docks and processing infrastructure, not a quaint backdrop. This is a feature, not a flaw: it tells the visitor they are in a real place, not a curated one. Source: [CLUI — Omega Protein Plant](https://clui.org/ludb/site/omega-protein-plant-port-reedville); Rivah Visitor's Guide.

### Skipjack Race
- No annual "Reedville Skipjack Race" was confirmed by web search. The Choptank Heritage Skipjack Race is held in Maryland each September and is unrelated. The Reedville Fishermen's Museum operates the skipjack Claud W. Somers for public cruises but no competitive race event was found. Source: search results (2026-05-30). Mark any mention of a race as UNVERIFIED — do not assert it.

### "Why this place feels different" insight
- Reedville is the only waterfront town on the Northern Neck where the industry that built it is still visibly operating. You can walk past a $1.5 million Victorian mansion and look across the creek at a functioning fish-reduction plant in the same glance. That coexistence — old money and working boats and ice cream and a museum — is not preserved history. It is an unfinished sentence. Source: synthesized from CLUI, Wikipedia, Rivah Visitor's Guide, and museum site; characterization is editorial framing for the Writer, not a quotable claim.
