# Research brief: winter-birding-northern-neck

## Angle & reader
A DC-area traveler who already knows the cottage in warm-weather months (or who
found the site through the osprey/wildlife posts) and is deciding whether a
winter stay is worth it. The pitch: winter is the Northern Neck's other bird
season, arguably the bigger one, watched from the same porch and dock, plus a
short list of nearby preserves and a refuge worth the drive. Frame winter as
the point of the trip, not a compromise on the summer version.

## Key facts

- **Property is in Northumberland County, VA, at approximately 37.95°N,
  76.45°W** (Hull Creek). Source: `src/lib/site.ts` (`PROPERTY.geo`,
  `PROPERTY.county`).
- **Verified drive times from the cottage** (`src/lib/site.ts`
  `DRIVE_TIMES`): Reedville 15 min, Kilmarnock 30 min, Irvington 45 min,
  **Warsaw 45 min**, Washington DC 165 min. Use these verbatim; do not restate
  from memory.
- **Hughlett Point Natural Area Preserve** — Northumberland County (same
  county as the cottage), coordinates 37°44'17"N 76°18'44"W, nearest named
  place Hardings, VA, on a peninsula near Kilmarnock. Managed by Virginia DCR.
  Has a parking area, trails, boardwalk, and a viewing platform. Documented
  habitat: tidal and non-tidal wetlands, undeveloped beach, dunes, upland
  forest. Documented winter wildlife: **an important wintering area for
  migrating waterfowl and songbirds; provides habitat for bald eagles, osprey,
  and northern harriers.** Sources do not specifically name tundra swans at
  this site — do not claim swans here. Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughlett_Point_Natural_Area_Preserve),
  [Virginia DCR](https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/natural-heritage/natural-area-preserves/hughlett),
  [Virginia DWR](https://dwr.virginia.gov/vbwt/sites/hughlett-point-natural-area-preserve/).
  **UNVERIFIED — do not state as fact: exact drive time/mileage from the
  cottage or Heathsville.** No source gives a number; straight-line distance
  from the property's coordinates is roughly 16 miles, which in this rural,
  two-lane-road part of the Neck is not a reliable stand-in for drive time.
  Write around it qualitatively (e.g. "a short drive, same county as the
  cottage") rather than inventing a minute count.
- **Dameron Marsh Natural Area Preserve** — 316 acres, Northumberland County
  (same county as the cottage), coordinates 37°46'55"N 76°17'57"W, near
  Kilmarnock. Managed by Virginia DCR. Has a parking area, hiking trails,
  boardwalks, and a small-watercraft boat launch. Documented winter wildlife:
  marshes offer **"superb views of bald eagle, brown pelican, wintering
  waterfowl, and wading birds."** A fallow field draws fall migrant songbirds
  and raptors including Merlin. Sources do not name tundra swans here either —
  do not claim swans at this site. Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dameron_Marsh_Natural_Area_Preserve),
  [Virginia DCR](https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/natural-heritage/natural-area-preserves/dameron),
  [eBird hotspot](https://ebird.org/hotspot/L421042?yr=cur).
  **UNVERIFIED — exact drive time/mileage from the cottage.** Same caveat as
  Hughlett Point: no sourced number; straight-line distance from the
  property's coordinates is roughly 14 miles. Write around it qualitatively.
- **Belle Isle State Park** — 1632 Belle Isle Rd, Lancaster, VA 22503. **Seven
  miles of shoreline on the Rappahannock River**, plus access to Mulberry and
  Deep creeks. Over 100 documented bird species. Confirmed seasonal pattern:
  **osprey are present roughly spring through October; once osprey migrate
  out, waterfowl move in, including ducks, Canada geese, and tundra swans.**
  This is the one location in this brief with a sourced tundra-swan claim —
  use it here, not at Hughlett Point or Dameron Marsh. Watch House Trail
  crosses grassland, open field, edge habitat, and marsh. Sources:
  [Virginia DCR](https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/state-parks/belle-isle),
  [Virginia's River Realm](https://www.virginiasriverrealm.com/places/belle-isle-state-park/).
  Driving directions from Kilmarnock (Route 3 W to Lively, then SR 201, then
  SR 354) put it roughly 9 miles past Kilmarnock, i.e. **on the order of
  Kilmarnock's verified 30-minute drive time from the cottage, plus some
  additional minutes** — treat that "plus some additional minutes" qualifier
  as load-bearing; do not round it down to a specific number.
- **Rappahannock River Valley National Wildlife Refuge** — administrative
  office/Wilna unit at 336 Wilna Road, **Warsaw, VA** 22572. The refuge is
  non-contiguous, with units spread across King George, Caroline, Essex,
  Westmoreland, and Richmond counties, so "the refuge" is not a single point
  on a map. Because the headquarters unit is in Warsaw, and Warsaw is already
  a **verified 45-minute drive from the cottage** (`DRIVE_TIMES`), it's fair
  to say the refuge's Wilna unit is about the same drive as Warsaw. Do not
  imply every unit of the refuge is that close; the outlying units (e.g. near
  King George) are farther. Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappahannock_River_Valley_National_Wildlife_Refuge),
  [U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service](https://www.fws.gov/refuge/rappahannock-river-valley/visit-us).
- **Northern Neck Audubon Society (nnvas.org)** — real organization, runs
  bird walks "usually held on the second Monday and last Saturday of the
  month," open to all experience levels, locations/times posted on their
  site. They also run a **winter waterfowl census** ("Northern Neck and
  Middle Peninsula Winter Waterfowl Count") covering the lower
  Rappahannock-Potomac rivers and adjacent lands. One documented February
  walk (Kendale Farm, Essex County) reported Tundra Swan, Canada Geese,
  Northern Harriers, and Bald Eagles among sightings — useful color for what
  a winter walk turns up, but Kendale Farm itself is in Essex County, farther
  from the cottage than the four sites above; mention the society and its
  winter walks/count generically, don't imply Kendale Farm is nearby. Source:
  [nnvas.org](https://www.nnvas.org/events).
- **"250+ species" claim** — sourced: the Northern Neck (between the Potomac
  and Rappahannock, next to the Chesapeake Bay) is described as home to
  **over 250 documented bird species**, attributed to habitat diversity and
  conservation efforts, with the Northern Neck Audubon Society cited as
  maintaining the record. Usable as stated, attributed loosely to "the
  region's birders" rather than over-citing.
- **Tundra swan season** — hundreds winter on the Chesapeake and tidal
  Potomac from **November through mid-March**, arriving in November, with
  numbers building and peaking around **December into January**. Do not
  state a hard "Dec-Feb only" window; the accurate range is wider (Nov-Mar)
  with a December-January peak. Source:
  [Bay Journal](https://www.bayjournal.com/columns/bay_naturalist/tundra-swans-en-route-to-winter-home-on-chesapeake/article_59535994-f979-50bd-97e6-9009270ec7ac.html),
  [Chesapeake Bay News](https://www.chesapeake-bay.org/waterfowl/tundra-swan/).
- **Existing site facts to reuse, not restate from memory**: hot tub is on
  the patio beside the porch, open year-round (`STANDOUT_AMENITIES`); two
  screened porches, one over the water; west-facing sunsets (seasonal, May
  to September, so don't lean on this one for a winter post).
